Research prototypes

Two platforms with different jobs, tried on four kinds of asset

EarthState Twin builds an asset's model, twin and capacity check from its drawings and records, through a conversation. Standfast looks after assets in service: hazard scenarios, collapse margins, strengthening and maintenance priorities. Towers and overhead lines came first, and bridges are next. WaterState and FacilityNexus apply the same method to pumping stations and to buildings. In all four, every number comes from a calculation that can be traced to its inputs, and an engineer signs each key step. The AI drafts; engineers decide.

Prototype 01 · Build, through a conversation

EarthState Twin

First case: ageing steel towers. Bridges next.

Can an asset's model, twin and capacity check be built from its drawings and records, with an engineer signing each step?

EarthState Twin works through a conversation. From an asset's drawings and records it builds a member-level model and runs its own checks before the model is used: equilibrium under gravity, a stiffness matrix without mechanisms, natural frequencies.

Sensors are registered on the members they sit on, the model is calibrated on one event and validated on another it has not seen, and every member is checked to EN 1993-1-1. Each step goes to an engineer to confirm. Transmission towers were the first case; bridges are next, by changing the structural model and the discipline rules.

Prototype 02 · Assets in service

Standfast

First case: overhead lines. Bridges next.

For assets in service, how close is each structure to collapse under a hazard, and what should be strengthened or maintained first?

Standfast looks after assets in service, on a 3D model of the network. It runs a storm or another hazard scenario across the network and analyses each structure under the load it would see at its own position, so the margin before collapse is known structure by structure.

A collapse sweep finds each structure's limit, strengthening options are compared side by side, and the maintenance priorities and the report go to an engineer to review. Overhead lines were the first case; the same approach is being extended to bridges.

Prototype 03 · The method applied to water

WaterState

Pumping stations

What maintenance does each failure mode of a pumping station need, and what should be done in the 72 hours before heavy rain?

WaterState applies reliability-centred maintenance to a pumping station. Each failure mode is listed with its effect and scored (FMECA), then taken through the RCM decision questions to a maintenance task, and the path taken is shown.

The AI drafts the reasoning from the work-order history. An engineer accepts or edits each answer, and the sign-off is recorded. A second view combines the rainfall forecast with wet-well level, pump current and vibration to rank the risk of a spill over the next 72 hours.

Prototype 04 · The method applied to buildings

FacilityNexus

Buildings and estates in operation

Can one twin hold the events, the equipment health and the operating knowledge of a building, so that every answer comes with its source?

FacilityNexus puts events, work orders, equipment health, energy and the operations knowledge base on one 3D model of the building. Each sensor point sits on the room or the unit it measures, so a complaint about a warm office can be traced to the supply branch behind it.

The assistant answers only from platform data and the operator's own procedures. Conclusions on safety and cost go to a person for review.

Laboratory and testbeds

Where the methods are tested before they reach an asset

Physical platforms and instrumented workflows, with what each one has been used for.

Physical platform

1:10 scale intelligent building-machine testbed

A scaled construction-machine platform in the structural engineering laboratory at Southeast University, commissioned in December 2025 and accepted with all 22 performance criteria met. It couples a dual-arm collaborative robot and PLC control with a digital twin, so a construction sequence can be run physically and in simulation at the same time and the two compared. My group uses the platform; it was procured by the university, not by me.

Software stack

Layered robotics and AI development environment

A seven-layer research stack running from operating system and middleware through physics simulation and synthetic data generation to policy learning and on-board deployment, built on ROS 2 and NVIDIA Isaac Sim with domain randomisation and sim-to-real correction. It exists so that a control approach can fail in simulation a thousand times before it is allowed near a real structure.

Instrumented workflow

Reality capture to structural assessment

Terrestrial laser scanning through point-cloud processing to a parametric BIM model and then to finite element analysis. Run end to end on a real bridge, this chain reached an Intersection over Union of up to 91% against critical structural outlines and identified 83% of primary components automatically. The numbers and the paper are on the Q&A page.

Instrumented workflow

Virtual trial assembly of prefabricated steel

Scan, model and analyse prefabricated components so the fit-up check happens before anything reaches site. On an experimental bridge the geometric prediction deviated by less than 1/1800 of the span, and predicted mean stress came within 90% of measured. Point-cloud registration accuracy, not the modelling, is what limits the whole chain.
